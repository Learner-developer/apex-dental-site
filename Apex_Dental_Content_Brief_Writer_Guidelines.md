# Apex Dental & Orthodontic Clinic — Content Brief & Writer Guidelines

## ⚠️ Read this section first — advertising compliance

Dr. Jyothi Sajjan practices under a real Karnataka State Dental Council registration, and dentists in India fall under the Dental Council of India's Code of Ethics on advertising. This isn't legal advice (worth a quick check with her rather than treating this as final), but based on what the code covers, three things in the current data pull are worth softening or confirming before they go live publicly:

1. **"Best Dental Clinic" language.** It's literally in her Google Business name, and it's tempting to use as a headline. The DCI code restricts unverifiable superlative claims ("best," "top") without a credential basis. Her real numbers (4.97★, 482 reviews, MDS-qualified orthodontist) are stronger and unimpeachable anyway — **lead with verifiable facts, not the superlative**, throughout this brief.
2. **Before-and-after photos of identifiable patients.** This is the highest-risk section on the site. The code restricts using patient treatment outcomes in advertising. Practically: confirm she has consent to use these specific images publicly before the gallery goes live on the public link — if unconfirmed, keep it as a password-gated section shown only live during the pitch call, not published on the open demo URL.
3. **Discount/percentage-off offer language** ("10% off Zirconia Crowns"). Price advertising in certain formats is restricted; indicative/informational pricing is more commonly accepted than promotional discount framing. The copy below reframes these as "Free Consultation" offers rather than percentage discounts, which is both safer and, frankly, converts better anyway (a free-consultation CTA is a smaller ask than a discount-driven one).

None of this means don't use the material — it means lead with what's unambiguously solid (the numbers, the credentials, the facility, short verified-linked testimonials) and treat the outcome-photo gallery as something to greenlight with her directly, not assume.

## Voice & Style Rules

**Persona**: written by someone who works at the clinic and genuinely knows the patients — warm, plain-spoken, competent. Not a marketing agency voice, not a hospital-brochure voice.

**Reading level**: aim for a 7th-8th grade reading level. Short sentences. If a sentence needs a comma to explain a comma, split it into two sentences.

**Voice**: active voice only. "Dr. Jyothi treats crowding cases in under a year," not "crowding cases are treated by Dr. Jyothi in under a year."

**Formatting**: paragraphs max 3 sentences. Bulleted lists for anything 3+ items, not comma-chained sentences.

**Banned words/phrases** — do not use, anywhere on the site: *delve, elevate, unlock/unlocking, journey (as in "smile journey"), state-of-the-art, cutting-edge, seamless, holistic, tailored (overused — say "personalized" or be specific instead), world-class, best-in-class, revolutionize, transform your smile (cliché-worn), in today's world, when it comes to.*

**Numbers over adjectives, wherever possible**: "482 reviews, 4.97 stars" beats "highly rated." "7-11 months" beats "fast." This is also the compliance-safe move (verifiable facts vs. unverifiable claims), so it does double duty.
